    How to squat

    Side view

    Six clean reps, then the coach notices the ascent slowing. Footage: Cavemantraining, CC BY-SA 4.0; the overlay and voice are this app.

    Facing the camera

    Half squats, so the thighs go red and the coach asks for more depth. Footage: Gareth Myles (GM-FITNESS), CC BY.

    Set up

    1. Camera 2–3 m away, side-on or facing you, feet in frame. Side-on shows depth and lean; facing the camera shows knee tracking and balance.
    2. Feet about shoulder-width apart, toes turned out a little, weight spread across the whole foot.
    3. Stand tall for a second so the app learns your standing height.

    The rep

    1. Brace your core and sit back and down, as if onto a chair behind you. Knees track over the toes.
    2. Keep your chest up. A forward lean is normal; folding over is not.
    3. Go down until the hip crease is level with the knee. That is the 0 line on the depth gauge.
    4. Drive up through the whole foot to a full stand. The rep counts when you are back at the top.

    What the coach watches

    • Shallow: the hips never reached knee level, so the rep is listed but not counted.
    • Forward lean: torso more than 40° from vertical at the bottom.
    • Knees in: knees narrower than the ankles on the way up.
    • Uneven: weight shifted to one side.
    • Tempo: a rushed descent, or reps slowing down as you tire.

    How it works and references

    How a rep is judged

    • Pose: MediaPipe Pose Landmarker finds 33 body points per frame [1]. The app uses the leg the camera sees best and works from the side, the front or in between: the view is detected from shoulder width against torso height.
    • Hip travel counts reps: how far the hip drops, as a fraction of standing leg length (hip height above the ankle, so a panning camera does not fake a rep). A rep starts past 15% and ends back under 8%. The 2D knee angle only confirms that the legs actually bend, so stepping toward the camera is not a rep, and every visible leg must bend, so a leg lift is not one either.
    • Depth is where the hip crease sits against the knee at the bottom, in thigh lengths: 0 is level with the knee (parallel), negative is above. Attempts that never travel 22% of leg length are listed but not counted.
    • Lean (side view) is the torso angle from vertical on the worst frame. Knees (front view) is knee width over ankle width, so knees caving in show as a low ratio. Balance (front view) is the hip line's tilt minus the shoulder line's tilt, which cancels the tilt perspective adds and leaves real weight shift.
    • Not a squat: bending over without sitting down (lean over 80°, or over 60° with shallow depth), a hallucinated hip below the frame, a rep that lasts under half a second or over 15 s, or travel over 90% of leg length, which only a camera cut produces.
    • Image shape: MediaPipe gives x and y as fractions of width and height, so x is scaled by the video's width/height before measuring angles.

    Squat types

    Both use the same counting logic; they differ only in how depth is judged.

    What the standards say

    • Depth: competition and test rules define a full squat by the hip crease passing below the top of the knee, which is the 0 line of the depth gauge [2]. A parallel squat (thigh horizontal) is the usual training target [3].
    • Knees: knees tracking over the toes, not collapsing inward (valgus), is the most common form cue in screening tests [4].
    • Lean: a forward trunk lean is normal in a squat; excessive lean with a rounded back shifts load to the spine. No standard gives a number in degrees, so the 40°/55° limits are this app's starting points.

    Calibration notes

    • The counting rules were tuned on 16 Creative Commons clips (front, side, three-quarter and low angles, bodyweight, goblet, racked and overhead squats) and one webcam session with three testers. Every clean set counts exactly; the only misses are on footage that cuts away mid-rep.
    • This page runs the same rules as the Python reference in squat/; on identical landmark input the two agree on every clip (17 of 17).
    • Depth and knee tracking need the knees and feet actually in frame. From a desk-height laptop camera the app still counts, but those two gauges switch off rather than guess.

    Limits

    • 2D only: back rounding and bar path are not checked.
    • Loose clothing, poor light or a very close camera reduce landmark accuracy, especially at the feet.
    • Not medical advice or a certified coaching tool.
