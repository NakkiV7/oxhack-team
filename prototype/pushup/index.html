    How it works and references

    How a rep is judged

    • Pose: MediaPipe Pose Landmarker finds 33 body points per frame [1]. The app uses the side the camera sees best (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle).
    • Elbow angle (shoulder - elbow - wrist) counts reps: it must drop below the variation's depth line, then rise back above the lockout line. Reps that never reach depth are listed but not counted.
    • Body line (shoulder - hip - ankle, or knee for knee push-ups) checks the body is straight. 180° is perfectly straight. Each rep is scored on its worst frame.
    • Sag or pike: if the line is bent, the hip position relative to the shoulder-ankle line decides "hips sagging" or "hips too high".
    • Image shape: MediaPipe gives x and y as fractions of width and height, so x is scaled by the video's width/height before measuring angles. Without this a horizontal body on a wide video looks more bent than it is [7].

    Push-up types

    All four use the same counting logic; they differ in body position, which joint the body line is measured to, and the angle limits. Hands-elevated (incline) and knee push-ups put less load through the hands than the standard push-up [6].

    Depth and lockout for the standard push-up follow published test rules (below). No published angle limits were found for wall, incline or knee push-ups, so those values are this app's own starting points and need tuning on real clips.

    What the standards say

    • Depth: elbows bent to at least 90°, chest close to the floor, shoulders, hips and knees in line [2]. US Army rule: lower until the upper arms are at least parallel to the ground [3].
    • Lockout: arms fully extended at the top, or the rep does not count [3].
    • Body line: "a generally straight line from your shoulders to your ankles" [3]. No standard gives a number in degrees. One pose-estimation study used 140° as its straight-body limit, 140° elbow for the top position and 15° maximum torso-to-ground angle at the bottom [4]. A rule-based 2D system used 110° or less as its "down" cutoff [5].

    Calibration notes

    • On a demonstration clip with good form (standard push-up, side view), the corrected body line was 167° median and 160° at its worst frames; the elbow went down to about 60°.
    • The standard body-line limit (165°) is stricter than that clip, so some good reps can still be flagged. It stays unchanged until a deliberate hips-sagging clip has been tested.

    Limits

    • 2D side view only: elbow flare and shoulder-blade movement are not checked.
    • Camera position changes accuracy a lot. In one study a phone counted up to 85.7% of push-ups from a diagonal view at 90 to 180 cm, and 20% from the front at 360 cm [8].
    • Not medical advice or a certified coaching tool.
